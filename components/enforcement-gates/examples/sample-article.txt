The Lantern Festival of Gray Harbor

Every autumn, the people of Gray Harbor carry paper lanterns down to the
waterfront and set them adrift on the evening tide. The festival began, the
old harbor keeper says, as a way to thank the sea for a safe season: each
lantern carries a small folded wish, and each wish is watched until its
light fades beyond the breakwater.

This year the tide came in higher than usual, and the lanterns drifted in a
long glowing line past the fishing boats. Children counted them from the
pier while the harbor bell rang once for every boat that had returned home.
By midnight the last lantern had slipped out of sight, and the harbor was
quiet again, waiting for another year.
